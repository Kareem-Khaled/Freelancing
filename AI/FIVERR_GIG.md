## The tension in merging (read this first)

Your own analysis is correct: Fiverr punishes generic. *"I will build an AI
agent"* drowns in a pool of thousands.

But merging four services into one gig **recreates that exact problem** if done
carelessly — *"I will build AI agents, RAG, document processing and support
automation"* is more generic than any of the four alone.

**The fix:** one specific promise as the spine, with the other capabilities
appearing as *scope inside tiers*, never as competing identities.

The spine that genuinely unites all three of your projects is not a technology.
It is a guarantee:

> **AI that handles your documents and tickets — and proves it isn't making
> things up.**

- Knowledge assistant → every answer carries a **verified citation**
- Invoice extraction → every total is **checked by deterministic arithmetic**
- Support triage → every decision passes **escalate-only business rules**

Three products, one promise: *verification, not trust*. That is your moat, and
almost nobody in that crowded pool is selling it.

## Requirements

These appear after the buyer orders. Fiverr already asks the generic questions
(deadline, brief), so every question here earns its place by being something
**only this project needs** — and each one prevents a specific, real failure.

Add each as a separate question in Fiverr's Requirements tab. Type and
required/optional are noted.

---

### 1. Free text — REQUIRED

> **Upload 5-10 sample documents, or describe them: what type are they (policy,
> contract, invoice, manual), and roughly how many in total?**

*Why:* Scope and price hinge on volume, and document type changes the chunking
strategy. A contract and an invoice need different handling.

---

### 2. Multiple choice — REQUIRED

> **Are your documents text-based or scanned?**
>
> - Text-based (I can select and copy text in the PDF)
> - Scanned images (photos or scans of paper)
> - A mix of both
> - Not sure — I'll upload samples

*Why:* This is the single biggest predictor of accuracy. Scans need OCR, which is
lossy. Asking up front lets you set expectations before work starts instead of
explaining a disappointing result afterwards.

---

### 3. Free text — REQUIRED

> **Write 5 real questions your team would actually ask these documents — in
> your own words, exactly as a colleague would ask them.**
>
> *Example: "What's our refund window for enterprise customers?" rather than
> "refund policy".*

*Why:* This is the most valuable question in the list. It becomes your accuracy
test, so acceptance is measured against their words, not yours. The phrasing
note matters — real users ask in natural language, and that is precisely what
separates a working system from one that only answers questions phrased like the
documents.

---

### 4. Multiple choice — REQUIRED

> **Where should this run?**
>
> - Cloud is fine (OpenAI/Anthropic API — fastest, lowest setup cost)
> - Must stay on our servers (documents cannot leave our network)
> - Not sure — advise me

*Why:* Changes the entire architecture, and it's the question clients most often
forget until late. On-premise is a Premium-tier requirement.

---

### 5. Multiple choice — REQUIRED

> **Who will use it?**
>
> - Internal staff only
> - Customers / public-facing
> - Both

*Why:* Public-facing raises the bar on refusal behaviour and tone. Worth knowing
before building, not after.

---

### 6. Multiple choice — OPTIONAL

> **What languages are your documents in?**
>
> - English only
> - Arabic only
> - English + Arabic
> - Other (tell me below)

*Why:* You support English + Arabic OCR. Anything else needs a different model
and should be quoted separately.

---

### 7. Free text — OPTIONAL

> **Have you tried an AI tool on these documents before? If so, what went
> wrong?**

*Why:* The highest-signal question in the set. Market research showed clients
are frequently burned by previous developers — *"two different developers have
attempted the project but have been unable to achieve the required accuracy."*
Their answer tells you exactly what to demo first, and asking it signals you've
seen this failure before.

---

### 8. Free text — OPTIONAL

> **Anything I should know about the documents? Confidential sections, tables,
> handwriting, unusual formatting?**

*Why:* Tables are your known weak spot — extraction can misalign rows. Surfacing
that before starting turns a potential revision dispute into an expectation you
set honestly.

## FAQ

**Will it make things up?**
It's built specifically to avoid that. Answers come only from your documents,
every claim is cited, citations are verified after generation, and when your
documents don't contain the answer it says so rather than guessing. On my
benchmark: zero fabricated citations, 100% of off-topic questions refused.

**Can it handle scanned PDFs?**
Yes, via OCR (Standard and Premium), English and Arabic. Be aware OCR is lossy —
accuracy depends on scan quality, and I'll tell you honestly after seeing samples.

**Do my documents leave my network?**
Only if you want them to. Premium includes fully on-premise deployment with a
local model — no external API calls at all.

**What if the answers aren't accurate enough?**
Standard and Premium include an accuracy report run against *your* questions, so
you see real numbers before sign-off. Revisions are for tuning against those
results.

**How many documents can it handle?**
Basic 25, Standard 300, Premium unlimited. Retrieval stays fast well beyond that —
search remains in the millisecond range at 100,000 passages.

**Can it do more than answer questions?**
Yes — Premium adds structured data extraction (invoices, forms → JSON/Excel with
validation) and ticket/email triage with automatic escalation rules.

**Which AI model?**
Your choice: OpenAI, Anthropic, or a local open model for privacy. I'll recommend
based on your accuracy, cost and privacy constraints.
